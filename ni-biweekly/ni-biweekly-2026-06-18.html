Voice-Based Structured Nursing Documentation Using Automatic Speech Recognition and Large Language Models: Development and Evaluation Study.

Su MH, Wang WC, Hsu YM, Hou SY, Chuang SJ, Chang SS
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This study developed and tested a system combining speech recognition and large language models to turn nurses' spoken words into structured DART documentation within hospital information systems, using a code-switching speech corpus from emergency and ward settings. The fine-tuned system cut speech recognition errors from 44.79% to 6.67%, achieved an F1-score of 0.82 for DART classification, and 75.8% of participating nurses reported reduced workload. The abstract suggests this approach may help ease manual documentation burden and improve record completeness in clinical nursing workflows.

sebis at CRF Filling 2026: A Two-Stage Local LLM Pipeline for Medical CRF Filling

Katharina Sommer, Tristan Till, Florian Matthes
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers tested a local, privacy-preserving pipeline using the MedGemma-27B language model to pull structured data from unstructured clinical notes for a Case Report Form filling task. The two-stage system—separating detection from value extraction—reached a macro-F1 score of 0.55, placing second among locally-hosted open-source submissions. For nursing informatics, this suggests that on-site language model pipelines may reduce privacy risks while still extracting usable clinical data from EHR notes.

Deterministic Integrity Gates for LLM-Assisted Clinical Manuscript Preparation: An Auditable Biomedical Informatics Architecture

Yoojin Nam, Jinhoon Jeong, Namkug Kim
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
This study developed an architecture to catch errors—such as fake citations and wrong numbers—in manuscripts written with AI assistance. The system used 43 tools, including 21 rule-based checks, and detected all 27 planted defects with no false alarms, while a single AI reviewer caught only 11. For nursing informatics, this work highlights that rule-based verification may catch errors that AI alone misses when producing research manuscripts.

Association Between Electronic Health Record-Based Nursing Workload and Turnover: Retrospective Cohort Study.

Xia L, Lew D, Tessmer L, Eiden E, Lou S, Kannampallil T
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This study examined whether EHR audit log data could predict nurse turnover among 432 inpatient nurses at one academic medical center during 2022. Researchers found that a higher share of medication administration actions linked to greater odds of leaving, while more alert engagement and more years of experience linked to lower odds. The abstract suggests EHR use patterns may help identify nurses at higher risk of turnover and inform retention efforts.

Assessing Digital Literacy Among Nursing Faculty Under the Artificial Intelligence-Enhanced Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge Framework: Cross-Sectional Survey Analysis.

Zhao J, Hua X, Hu R, Chen C, Wu W, Zhang Y et al.
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This cross-sectional study surveyed 339 nursing faculty at eight universities in Fujian province, China, to measure their digital literacy using a framework built around AI-enhanced teaching knowledge. Faculty scored at a medium overall level, with the highest scores in digital awareness and the lowest in technical knowledge and skills; older age predicted lower scores across multiple dimensions. The findings highlight a skills gap that nursing informatics educators may want to consider when designing faculty development programs.

Informatics Competency and Technology Self-Efficacy Levels Among Undergraduate Nursing Students: Cross-Sectional Study.

Alnomasy N, Alrashedi H, Alsayed S, Pangket P, Alsayed R
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This cross-sectional study measured informatics competency and digital self-efficacy among 243 undergraduate nursing students at the University of Hail, Saudi Arabia. Students reported moderate informatics competency, while those with prior informatics training or frequent electronic health record use scored notably higher. The authors caution that single-site, self-report, and underpowered findings limit generalisability, and call for future multisite research to guide informatics education aligned with Saudi Vision 2030.

Toward Accessible Psychotherapy Training Using AI-Driven Interactive Patient Avatars

Pascal Riachi, Sofie Kamber, Stella Brogna, Andrew Gloster, Rafael Wampfler
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers built an AI system that lets psychotherapy trainees practice Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) through spoken dialogue with a virtual patient, with turn-by-turn automated feedback. Expert psychologists rated the virtual patient as highly realistic, and the model GPT-4o-mini closely matched human supervisor ratings across 49 transcripts. While focused on psychotherapy training, this work shows how AI-driven simulation and automated fidelity feedback could support clinical skill-building in informatics-enabled training environments.

Adult-Learning Newborn Medicine Curriculum Improves Knowledge in a Low-Resource Neonatal Unit in Sierra Leone

Mvula, M.; Amin, A.; Patil, M. S.; Valentine, G.; Mukarwego, B.; Wagner, S.; Dumbuya, I.; Lou, L.; Sanni, U.; Hansen, A.
medRxiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers tested a two-phase virtual and in-person neonatal medicine curriculum with 38 healthcare providers—mostly nurses—at a Special Care Baby Unit in Sierra Leone. Knowledge scores rose significantly in both phases (from roughly 52–56% to 85–92% correct, p<0.001), and nearly all participants rated the curriculum as very effective. Because nurses made up the majority of learners, findings suggest that structured virtual-plus-in-person education models may help build neonatal care capacity among nurses in low-resource settings.

Auricular Point Acupressure Self-Management (APA-SM) program for chronic musculoskeletal pain among rural populations: a protocol for a pragmatic, randomized controlled trial.

Kawi J, Bolin J, Wu H, Min J, Bora R, Pace S et al.
BMJ open · Jun 2026
This protocol describes a three-arm randomized controlled trial testing a smartphone-supported, needleless auricular point acupressure self-management program for chronic musculoskeletal pain in rural adults across Texas and South Carolina (n=693). Because this is a study protocol, no results are yet available. The trial's use of digital health tools, ecological momentary assessment, and electronic health records makes it relevant to nursing informatics researchers tracking technology-supported pain self-management.

Mind Companion: An Embodied Conversational Agent for Process-Based Psychotherapy

Sofie Kamber, Lukas Diebold, Pascal Riachi, Stella Brogna, Andrew Gloster, Rafael Wampfler
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers built and tested Mind Companion, an AI chatbot using large language models to deliver process-based psychotherapy, addressing long waitlists for mental health care. In evaluations involving 11 professional psychotherapists, one model configuration (GPT-5.2) received higher ratings than human therapist responses on measures like understanding and collaboration. For nursing informatics, this study raises questions about how AI-generated therapeutic responses might be monitored and integrated into clinician-supervised care workflows.

Coverage, Utilisation and Users of a Chat-Based Digital Primary Care Clinic in Publicly Funded Healthcare: A Registry-Based Observational Study in Finland

Dahlberg, A.; Kaartinen, T.; Jukarainen, S.; Orre, P.
medRxiv (preprint) · 2026
This Finnish registry study examined how a 24/7 chat-based digital clinic changed primary care use across nearly 2.8 million encounters from 2019 to 2025. Digital encounters grew from 19.6% to 29.8% of all contacts, with digital users being younger and having fewer recorded health conditions; nurses managed most cases without further follow-up, and 18% were escalated to a physician the same day. For nursing informatics, the data show the scale of nurse-led digital triage and its escalation patterns in a public system.

The voice of decompensation: an explainable machine learning approach to assessing heart failure severity.

Yang Y, Zhao B, Ying D, Han P, Wang Y, Liu K et al.
European journal of cardiovascular nursing · Jun 2026
Researchers recorded the voices of patients hospitalized with acute heart failure across four centres, then trained machine learning models to track changes in clinical status from admission to discharge using smartphone recordings. The best model reached 81% accuracy and 89% sensitivity, and cross-device testing showed consistent results across common smartphone brands. The abstract suggests this low-burden, non-invasive approach could support nurse-coordinated remote monitoring and triage after patients leave hospital.

Qingfeng Zhang, Yuanxiong Guo, Yanmin Gong
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers developed NeurMLLM, a multimodal AI framework that combines voice audio features, transcripts, and demographic data to stage Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases, tested on the Bridge2AI-Voice dataset. The system outperformed classical machine learning and existing large language model approaches on fine-grained disease staging. For nursing informatics, this points to potential for voice-based, accessible screening tools, though the study reports only technical performance and does not address clinical or real-world deployment.

LLM-Powered Personalized Glycemic Assessment in Type 2 Diabetes with Wearable Sensor Data

Yifan Gao, Yanmin Gong, Yun Shi, Yuanxiong Guo
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers developed GlyLLM, a large language model framework that combines continuous glucose monitor data, fitness tracker data, and personal health information to forecast blood glucose levels and classify diabetes in people with Type 2 diabetes. On the AI-READI dataset, GlyLLM outperformed traditional machine learning methods by about 14% in glucose forecasting and 13% in diabetes classification accuracy. For nursing informatics, this work suggests that combining wearable sensor data with structured patient metadata may improve personalized glycemic monitoring tools.

Lubna M. Abu Zohair, Marta Vallejo, MD Azher Uddin, John R. Woodward, Hind Zantout
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers applied an unsupervised machine learning framework to longitudinal clinical data from 302 people with Huntington's disease, aiming to identify disease stages in a data-driven way rather than relying on predefined clinical cut-offs. The framework identified four distinct disease stages with minimal overlap between groups. For nursing informatics, this approach suggests that graph-based learning tools applied to longitudinal patient data may help surface patient subgroups that standard staging methods could miss.

Zaifu Zhan, Shuang Zhou, Rui Zhang
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers tested a system where multiple AI language models check each other's reasoning to answer medical questions more accurately. The multi-agent approach reached an average accuracy of 0.820 across three benchmark datasets, outperforming both single-model and majority-vote methods. For nursing informatics, this suggests that having AI models evaluate each other's logic—rather than just tallying answers—may improve reliability in clinical decision-support tools, though this study used benchmarks, not real clinical settings.
