Voice-Based Structured Nursing Documentation Using Automatic Speech Recognition and Large Language Models: Development and Evaluation Study.

Su MH, Wang WC, Hsu YM, Hou SY, Chuang SJ, Chang SS
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
Researchers in Taiwan developed and tested a system combining speech recognition and a large language model to convert nurses' spoken notes into structured DART documentation within hospital information systems. After fine-tuning, speech recognition errors dropped from 44.79% to 6.67%, DART classification reached an F1-score of 0.82, and voluntary monthly note use doubled; 75.8% of the 120 participating nurses reported reduced workload and better record completeness. The abstract suggests this approach may help ease manual documentation burden for clinical nurses.

EHR & Workflows

Association Between Electronic Health Record-Based Nursing Workload and Turnover: Retrospective Cohort Study.

Xia L, Lew D, Tessmer L, Eiden E, Lou S, Kannampallil T
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
Researchers analyzed EHR audit logs from 432 inpatient nurses at one academic medical center to measure workload patterns and their link to turnover. Nurses with a higher share of medication administration actions were more likely to leave, while greater alert engagement and more years of experience were linked to lower turnover odds. The abstract suggests EHR activity patterns may help identify nurses at higher risk of leaving.

Workforce & Education

Assessing Digital Literacy Among Nursing Faculty Under the Artificial Intelligence-Enhanced Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge Framework: Cross-Sectional Survey Analysis.

Zhao J, Hua X, Hu R, Chen C, Wu W, Zhang Y et al.
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This cross-sectional study surveyed 339 nursing faculty across eight universities in Fujian, China, measuring digital literacy using an AI-enhanced framework. Overall scores were at a medium level, with digital awareness scoring highest and technical knowledge and skills scoring lowest; older age consistently predicted lower scores. The findings suggest nursing faculty may need targeted training to strengthen practical digital skills, particularly among senior staff.

Workforce & Education

Informatics Competency and Technology Self-Efficacy Levels Among Undergraduate Nursing Students: Cross-Sectional Study.

Alnomasy N, Alrashedi H, Alsayed S, Pangket P, Alsayed R
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This cross-sectional study measured informatics competency and digital technology self-efficacy among 243 undergraduate nursing students at the University of Hail, Saudi Arabia. Students reported moderate informatics competency and moderate-to-high digital self-efficacy, with higher informatics scores linked to prior informatics training and frequent electronic health record use. The authors caution that single-site, self-reported, and underpowered findings limit generalisability, and they call for future multisite and mixed-methods research to better inform nursing informatics education.

Auricular Point Acupressure Self-Management (APA-SM) program for chronic musculoskeletal pain among rural populations: a protocol for a pragmatic, randomized controlled trial.

Kawi J, Bolin J, Wu H, Min J, Bora R, Pace S et al.
BMJ open · Jun 2026
This study protocol describes a three-arm randomized controlled trial testing a smartphone-supported, needleless ear acupressure self-management program (APA-SM) for chronic musculoskeletal pain in rural adults across Texas and South Carolina. Because this is a protocol paper, no results are yet available. The trial's use of digital health tools, electronic health records, and implementation science methods makes it relevant to nursing informatics researchers interested in technology-supported pain self-management in underserved communities.

AI Methods & Evaluation

The voice of decompensation: an explainable machine learning approach to assessing heart failure severity.

Yang Y, Zhao B, Ying D, Han P, Wang Y, Liu K et al.
European journal of cardiovascular nursing · Jun 2026
Researchers recruited patients hospitalised with acute heart failure across four centres to develop and test a smartphone-based voice monitoring system. XGBoost models trained on acoustic features from speech recordings achieved up to 0.81 accuracy and 0.89 sensitivity in distinguishing clinical status changes during hospitalisation, and recordings performed consistently across different smartphone brands. The abstract suggests this tool could support nurse-coordinated post-discharge monitoring and remote triage as a low-burden, non-invasive approach.
