Prompt, Plan, Extract: Zero-Shot Agentic LLMs Workflows for Lung Pathology Extraction from Clinical Narratives

Aman Pathak, Cheng Peng, Mengxian Lyu, Ziyi Chen, Reema Solan, Sankalp Talankar
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
This study tested whether open-source large language models (LLMs), without any task-specific training, could pull key cancer data fields from lung surgery pathology reports. The best zero-shot model scored a Micro-F1 of 0.893, somewhat below the supervised baseline (0.960), but still accurately captured complex data like cancer stage. For nursing informatics, this points to a lower-cost way to automate data extraction for cancer registries, though the gap with supervised methods remains.

sebis at CRF Filling 2026: A Two-Stage Local LLM Pipeline for Medical CRF Filling

Katharina Sommer, Tristan Till, Florian Matthes
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers built and tested a two-stage pipeline using a locally run AI model (MedGemma-27B) to pull structured information from unstructured clinical notes without sending data to external servers. The system reached a macro-F1 score of 0.55, placing second among open-source, locally hosted entries in a 2026 shared task. For nursing informatics, this suggests that privacy-protecting, on-site language model pipelines may be a practical option for automating clinical form completion.

Deterministic Integrity Gates for LLM-Assisted Clinical Manuscript Preparation: An Auditable Biomedical Informatics Architecture

Yoojin Nam, Jinhoon Jeong, Namkug Kim
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
This study examined whether deterministic (rule-based) checks could catch errors in AI-generated clinical manuscripts better than asking an AI to review its own work. The system detected all 27 injected defects with no false positives, while a single AI reviewer caught only 11. For nursing informatics, this suggests that pairing AI writing tools with structured, rule-based verification may produce a more auditable trail for checking AI-assisted research documents.

Association Between Electronic Health Record-Based Nursing Workload and Turnover: Retrospective Cohort Study.

Xia L, Lew D, Tessmer L, Eiden E, Lou S, Kannampallil T
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This study examined whether EHR audit log data could predict nurse turnover among 432 inpatient nurses at a large academic medical center during 2022. Researchers found that nurses with a higher share of medication administration actions were more likely to leave, while greater alert engagement and more years of experience linked to lower turnover odds. The abstract suggests EHR activity patterns may help identify nurses at higher risk of leaving, which could inform retention efforts.

Assessing Digital Literacy Among Nursing Faculty Under the Artificial Intelligence-Enhanced Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge Framework: Cross-Sectional Survey Analysis.

Zhao J, Hua X, Hu R, Chen C, Wu W, Zhang Y et al.
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This cross-sectional survey measured digital literacy among 339 nursing faculty at eight universities in Fujian province, China, using a framework built around AI-enhanced teaching knowledge. Faculty scored at a medium overall level, with digital awareness rated highest and technical knowledge and skills rated lowest; older age negatively predicted multiple dimensions, while more teaching years positively predicted digital application ability. The findings highlight a skills gap in nursing education that the study's authors say warrants targeted training, especially for senior faculty.

Informatics Competency and Technology Self-Efficacy Levels Among Undergraduate Nursing Students: Cross-Sectional Study.

Alnomasy N, Alrashedi H, Alsayed S, Pangket P, Alsayed R
JMIR nursing · Jun 2026
This cross-sectional study measured informatics competency and digital technology self-efficacy among 243 undergraduate nursing students at the University of Hail, Saudi Arabia. Students reported moderate informatics competency and moderate-to-high technology self-efficacy, with higher competency scores linked to prior informatics training and frequent electronic health record use. The authors caution that the single-site, self-reported, and underpowered findings should guide future multisite research rather than draw firm conclusions.

Toward Accessible Psychotherapy Training Using AI-Driven Interactive Patient Avatars

Pascal Riachi, Sofie Kamber, Stella Brogna, Andrew Gloster, Rafael Wampfler
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers built an AI system that lets psychotherapy trainees practice Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) with a virtual patient, receiving turn-by-turn feedback on their responses. Testing across 49 therapy transcripts found that GPT-4o-mini best matched human supervisor ratings of ACT fidelity. While focused on psychotherapy training, this work shows how AI-driven simulation and automated feedback tools could support clinical skill-building in ways relevant to nursing informatics research.

Auricular Point Acupressure Self-Management (APA-SM) program for chronic musculoskeletal pain among rural populations: a protocol for a pragmatic, randomized controlled trial.

Kawi J, Bolin J, Wu H, Min J, Bora R, Pace S et al.
BMJ open · Jun 2026
This protocol describes a three-arm randomized controlled trial testing a smartphone-supported, self-managed ear acupressure program (APA-SM) for chronic musculoskeletal pain in 693 rural adults across Texas and South Carolina. Because this is a study protocol, no results are yet available. The trial's use of digital health tools, electronic health records, and implementation science methods makes it relevant to nursing informatics researchers studying technology-supported pain self-management in underserved communities.

Mind Companion: An Embodied Conversational Agent for Process-Based Psychotherapy

Sofie Kamber, Lukas Diebold, Pascal Riachi, Stella Brogna, Andrew Gloster, Rafael Wampfler
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
This study developed and tested Mind Companion, an AI chatbot designed to deliver psychotherapy support when human therapists are unavailable. Using real therapy session transcripts, 11 professional psychotherapists rated one AI model (GPT-5.2) higher than human therapist responses on understanding, collaboration, and therapeutic alignment. For nursing informatics, the system's built-in safety monitoring and clinician-oversight features represent design elements worth tracking as AI mental health tools move toward clinical settings.

The voice of decompensation: an explainable machine learning approach to assessing heart failure severity.

Yang Y, Zhao B, Ying D, Han P, Wang Y, Liu K et al.
European journal of cardiovascular nursing · Jun 2026
This multicentre study recorded voice samples from patients hospitalised with acute heart failure, using both professional devices and common smartphones, to train machine learning models that classify clinical status from admission to discharge. The best model reached 81% accuracy and 89% sensitivity, with smartphone recordings showing high consistency across brands. The authors suggest this low-burden, non-invasive approach could support nurse-coordinated remote monitoring after patients leave hospital.

Unifying Acoustic Features and Text with Multimodal LLMs for Neurodegenerative Screening

Qingfeng Zhang, Yuanxiong Guo, Yanmin Gong
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers developed NeurMLLM, a multimodal AI framework that combines voice audio features, transcripts, and demographic data to stage Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases. Tested on the Bridge2AI-Voice dataset, the system outperformed classical machine learning and existing large language model approaches for fine-grained disease staging. For nursing informatics, this highlights growing interest in voice-based, accessible screening tools, though this is a preprint and has not yet undergone peer review.

LLM-Powered Personalized Glycemic Assessment in Type 2 Diabetes with Wearable Sensor Data

Yifan Gao, Yanmin Gong, Yun Shi, Yuanxiong Guo
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers developed GlyLLM, a large language model framework that combines continuous glucose monitor data, wearable sensor readings, and personal health information to forecast blood glucose levels and classify diabetes in people with Type 2 diabetes. Using the AI-READI dataset, GlyLLM outperformed traditional machine learning methods by about 14% on glucose forecasting and 13% on diabetes categorization. For nursing informatics, this signals growing potential for AI tools that personalize glycemic monitoring by drawing on richer patient data.

Let LLMs Judge Each Other: Multi-Agent Peer-Reviewed Reasoning for Medical Question Answering

Zaifu Zhan, Shuang Zhou, Rui Zhang
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
This study tested a method where multiple AI language models answer medical questions independently, then review each other's reasoning before selecting the best answer. The approach outperformed single-model and voting-based methods, reaching an average accuracy of 0.820 across three medical question datasets. For nursing informatics researchers, this suggests that multi-agent AI designs that evaluate reasoning quality—not just answer agreement—may support more reliable clinical decision-support tools.

Deep Temporal Modeling and Ensemble Fusion for Multimodal Emotion Recognition from Physiological Signals

Desta Haileselassie Hagos, Saurav Keshari Aryal, Patrick Ymele-Leki, Anietie Andy, Legand L. Burge
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
This study tested deep learning models—LSTM, TCN, and Transformer—on a public dataset (WESAD) to recognize emotions from wrist and chest sensor signals. The ensemble method combining all three models reached 98.91% accuracy, with Transformer models performing best when using both sensor locations together. Findings suggest that combining multiple sensor types and model architectures may improve the accuracy of physiological stress and emotion monitoring systems.

A systematic investigation of molecular encoding methods for drug property predictions across neural network and Transformer encoder-based model

Sheng-Ya Chen, Shan-Ju Yeh
arXiv (preprint) · 2026
Researchers tested different ways to encode molecular structures as inputs for two neural network models predicting drug properties such as toxicity and blood-brain barrier permeability. Models using certain fingerprint types (MACCS and PubChem) achieved average AUC values above 0.9 and highlighted chemically meaningful molecular features through built-in attention weights. For nursing informatics, this work offers guidance on building more interpretable drug-property prediction tools, though the study focuses on chemistry rather than clinical or nursing workflows.
