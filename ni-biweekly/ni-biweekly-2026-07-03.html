Using AI to identify in-hospital falls: a comparative analysis with incident reports and manual chart review.

Leurs WL, Groeneveld M, Haan B, Lammers LA, Bouwman AR, van der Linden CM
BMJ open quality · Jun 2026
This retrospective study at a large Dutch teaching hospital tested an AI algorithm to detect in-hospital falls in nursing records, comparing it with incident reports and manual chart review among 1,623 patients. The AI algorithm detected more falls than incident reports (79.2% vs 36.8%) and cut review time by 92.6%. This suggests AI-assisted review could help nurses catch more falls with less administrative burden, supporting more timely prevention efforts.

Evaluating Artificial Intelligence-Generated Nursing Care Plans: A Scenario-Based Comparative Study of Accuracy, Completeness, Quality, and Readability.

Yilmaz Akyaz D, Esim D, Cakir GN, Basut EA, Tufekci S, Konyar M et al.
Journal of clinical nursing · Jul 2026
Researchers compared ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek on generating nursing care plans for ten clinical scenarios across five nursing specialties, using standardized NANDA-I, NIC, and NOC taxonomies. All three tools produced moderate-to-high quality plans, with DeepSeek showing slightly better accuracy and completeness, and surgical scenarios performing best. However, all outputs were incomplete and written at a college reading level, suggesting these tools can only serve as preliminary drafts needing expert review before clinical use.

The Impact of Electronic Health Records on Nurses and Nursing Care in Low- and Middle-Income Countries: A Scoping Review.

Osman W, Asah-Ofori S, Harris A, Yakong VN, Widger K, Chu CH
Nursing open · Jul 2026
This scoping review examined 41 studies (2015–2024) on how electronic health records affect nurses and nursing care in low- and middle-income countries. EHRs improved documentation and data access, but studies also reported data quality problems, workflow disruption, technostress, depersonalized care, and uneven research output across regions, especially Sub-Saharan Africa. The authors call for more theory-based, nurse-led research to guide context-sensitive digital health policy and practice.

Use of a Conversational Agent for Training Mental Health Professionals in Suicide Safety Planning: Pilot Feasibility and Acceptability Study.

Nobile B, Elyoseph Z, Gourguechonbuot E, Guyodo J, Garcia J, Levkovich I et al.
JMIR mental health · Jun 2026
This pilot study tested a GenAI-based chatbot simulator that trains nurses and nursing assistants in psychiatric units to create suicide safety plans. Twenty participants rated the tool as highly acceptable and moderately-to-highly realistic, and their willingness to manage patients with suicidal ideation increased after the session. This suggests conversational agents may offer a feasible training tool, though the authors call for larger controlled trials before drawing conclusions about training effectiveness or patient outcomes.

Future nurses' attitudes and anxiety toward artificial intelligence: A cross-sectional study.

Kanat C, Çetinkaya ZS, Durak A
Nurse education today · Jul 2026
This cross-sectional study surveyed 320 nursing students in Türkiye to explore how their attitudes toward AI relate to AI-related anxiety. Negative attitudes correlated with higher anxiety across measures, while positive attitudes and AI interest linked to lower anxiety; anxiety levels also varied by gender and rural versus urban residence. The findings suggest nursing curricula may benefit from structured AI education to build familiarity and reduce anxiety, though the study does not test whether such education would change outcomes.

Li Y, Chen S, Dong X, Lu X, Chen X, Bai D et al.

This mixed-methods systematic review combined 28 studies to explore nursing students' perceptions and attitudes toward using artificial intelligence in nursing education. This synthesis helps nursing educators and informatics researchers understand student attitudes when designing or introducing AI tools in nursing curricula.
