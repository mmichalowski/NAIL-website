This issue at a glance

This issue's 37 papers cluster around nurses' readiness for AI and clinical decision support testing. Several studies measure nursing students' and clinicians' AI literacy, anxiety, and attitudes, often finding mixed knowledge but generally positive views. A parallel set of papers tests prediction tools for triage, delirium, pressure injuries, and stroke mortality using nursing and bedside data, usually comparing machine learning against simpler scores. A third thread raises governance and ethics questions, covering intersectionality in AI design, ethics scales, professional organization roles, and frameworks for system-level oversight.

Papers grew from 12 to 37 (+25), with Workforce & Education remaining the leading topic, as it was last issue. AI Ethics & Governance rose in share, now including papers on governance frameworks, human rights advocacy, and concept analysis of AI justification, while Patient-Facing AI fell to a single paper this issue after being a rising thread last time. Clinical Decision Support expanded substantially, from 2 papers to 10, covering triage, delirium, pressure injury, and medication error tools. The prior issue's patient-facing chatbot and telephone follow-up comparisons are not continued this issue.

Development and Temporal Validation of a Logistic Regression-Based Risk Score for Identifying Life-Threatening Chest Pain at Emergency Department Triage: A Retrospective Cohort Study.

Xu D, Gao P, Xu Q, Nie H
Vascular health and risk management · 2026
Researchers in Hefei, China, built a simplified risk score to spot life-threatening chest pain at ED nurse triage, using only information available without lab results. Tested on 2,506 adults, the logistic regression score performed as well as more complex machine-learning models and mapped onto China's four-level triage scale. This gives nurses an interpretable tool to support—not replace—triage decisions, though wider testing is needed before routine use.

Nursing inquiry · Oct 2026
This interpretive description study interviewed 18 obstetric nurses in a physician-led Saudi maternity service about using AI-enhanced cardiotocography monitoring. Li G, Gong C

Researchers used nursing, emergency, and lab data from the first 24 hours to build a machine learning model predicting in-hospital death in 5,014 acute ischemic stroke patients across three hospitals. The CatBoost model and a simpler 10-feature nomogram both outperformed standard ICU scoring systems in internal and external testing, with strong accuracy and calibration. This suggests bedside nursing data, combined with early clinical information, can improve early mortality prediction tools for stroke patients.

Evaluating Large Language Model‐Assisted Emergency Triage: A Comparison of Acuity Assessments by GPT‐4 and Medical Experts.

Haim, Gal Ben Saban, Mor Barash, Yiftach Cirulnik, David Shaham, Amit Eisenman, Ben Zion Burshtein, Livnat Mymon, Orly Klang, Eyal Department of Emergency Medicine, Sheba Medical Center, Ramat‐Gan, Israel
Journal of Clinical Nursing (John Wiley & Sons, Inc.) · Oct2
Researchers compared GPT-4's Emergency Severity Index (ESI) scores to those of senior ED nurses and physicians for 100 adult emergency department patients, using the same patient data for both. GPT-4 assigned lower median ESI scores than human evaluators, suggesting it rated patients as more severe, and the two approaches weighed age and vital signs differently. Understanding Mothers' Experiences of Very Low Birth Weight Infants Across the Childbirth Trajectory in Japan: A Retrospective Analysis Using Natural Language Processing.

Kojima M, Yamamoto T, Ishii A, Nishimura T, Fujino H
Japan journal of nursing science : JJNS · Oct 2026
Researchers in Japan analyzed notebook records from 280 mothers of very low birth weight infants, written during a support class between 2003 and 2018, using natural language processing to study experiences from pregnancy through the infant's first year. Topic modeling found apologetic or self-blaming language around the first meeting with their baby and the NICU stay, shifting to routine caregiving concerns by 6-12 months postpartum. This descriptive overview may support future qualitative and clinical research on maternal experiences during this period.

Zeng Y, Chen Y, Yu J, Yang J, Fu Y, Chen J et al.
International journal of nursing studies · Oct 2026
Researchers tested whether large language models (ChatGPT and DeepSeek) could analyze interview data from 15 postoperative bone tumor patients experiencing kinesiophobia, comparing results to a human researcher's coding. The AI models identified the same four themes as the human analyst, showing strong agreement (Cohen's κ = 0.89) while cutting coding time. This suggests LLMs can support faster qualitative analysis, but human judgment is still needed to interpret emotional nuances and context.

Developing and Evaluating the Use of ChatGPT as a Screening Tool for Nurses Conducting Structured Literature Reviews: Proof of Concept Study Results.

Mudd, Alexandra Conroy, Tiffany Voldbjerg, Siri Lygum Goldschmied, Anita Feo, Rebecca Schuwirth, Lambert Flinders University, Adelaide, Australia
Journal of Clinical Nursing (John Wiley & Sons, Inc.) · Oct2
This proof-of-concept study tested four versions of ChatGPT against a human researcher for screening 400 nursing education abstracts from four databases. ChatGPT-3.5 Turbo screened fastest and missed fewer relevant studies than the human researcher, while newer versions (GPT-4, 4 Turbo, 4 omni) missed more and struggled with nuanced nursing concepts. This suggests LLMs may speed up literature screening, but nurses need to test and report on their performance before relying on them.

Researchers developed and tested a new scale to measure Chinese nurses' ethical views on AI use in clinical practice, using a two-phase process with factor analysis and structural modelling. This scoping review examined 33 studies (over 9000 nurses across 10 countries) on nurses' knowledge, attitudes, literacy, anxiety, and readiness regarding AI in clinical practice. Nurse education today · Oct 2026
This systematic review and meta-analysis examined how large language models (LLMs) perform on nursing licensure exam questions, pooling 12 studies with 13,870 multiple-choice questions across ten LLMs. Pooled accuracy was 69.6%, with newer or nursing-customised models reaching up to 93.6%, though performance varied widely and models struggled with ethics and psychosocial content. This suggests LLMs may support low-stakes learning activities but are not yet reliable enough for unmoderated, high-stakes licensure decisions.

The Invisible Workforce: An Algorithmic Audit of Nursing, Primary Care, and Holistic Gaps in AI-Generated Survivorship Plans.

İnan M, Akar İnan S, Bumin G, Aypak C
Seminars in oncology nursing · Oct 2026
Researchers audited 50 ChatGPT-5.1 responses using a simulated advanced ovarian cancer survivor to see how survivorship plans credited different healthcare professionals. Medical oncology received full credit (100%), but nursing, primary care, and genetic counseling were rarely named, even though nursing-related tasks appeared in 96% of responses. The findings suggest nurses should review AI-generated plans to fix missing referrals and simplify reading level, since responses averaged an 11th-grade reading difficulty.

Martin-Loeches I, Cai H
Medical sciences (Basel, Switzerland) · Sep 2026
This paper addresses a problem in critical care informatics: sentiment analysis of ICU clinical notes predicts mortality only marginally better than existing severity scores. The authors propose a new target instead—a 'narrative-physiology discordance score' that measures gaps between clinician-documented risk and data-driven risk, with formal controls and a staged evaluation plan involving clinician adjudication. This reframing could help nursing informatics researchers design tools that flag disagreement needing review, rather than just predicting death.

Conscious justification in using artificial intelligence: A concept analysis for global nursing practice.

Ramos MD, Smith R, Allen R, Giarratano G
International journal of nursing studies · Oct 2026
This concept analysis used Walker and Avant's method to define 'conscious justification'—the deliberate, reasoned explanation of AI-mediated decisions in nursing, addressed to affected stakeholders. The authors identified six key attributes, including normative transparency, relational answerability, and epistemic humility, plus antecedents and consequences like improved trust but also risks like justification theater. This framework helps nursing informatics build global standards for explaining AI decisions responsibly across different healthcare systems and cultures.
